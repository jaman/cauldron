# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

- The app's own images, colours and symbols: `assets:` names an Xcode asset catalog, which Apple's systems use as it is (its `AccentColor` the app's accent) and Android takes converted into drawables, colours (with their dark appearance, and the system colours a colour set refers to) and vector drawables (`CauldronNative.Build.Assets`). `CauldronNative.UI.image/2` shows one with `{:asset, name}`, labels and icons take `asset:`, and colours may be `{:asset, name}`; the app's Kotlin finds them with `cauldronAsset/1` and `cauldronAssetColour/1`.
- `CauldronNative.UI.gauge/2` (linear, circular and segmented, its value text or pictures), the `gradient` modifier, `frame`'s `min_width` and `min_height`, `zstack/2`'s corner alignments, `menu_bar_extra/3` (an item of the Mac's menu bar opening a window of controls), and table cells that are controls with fixed-width columns (`table/3`).
- `{:cauldron_native, :window, %{width, height, compact}}`: a `CauldronNative.App` learns its window's size and whether it is compact, when it shows and as it changes.
- The Mac window's first and least size (`window:`).
- Widgets: the Lock Screen's sizes on iOS (`:circular`, `:rectangular`, `:inline`), content per size, timelines (`CauldronNative.Widget.timeline/2`), and pictures, gauges, gradients and the app's colours in both renderers; Android redraws a widget when its next entry is due. A widget's content is kept in the App Group's `Library/Widgets` folder.
- Live Activities and Live Updates count down to `ends_at`, show the app's own `image`, colours and `url`.
- `CauldronNative.Purchases`: products' amounts, periods, monthly prices and introductory offers; `subscription/2`, `transactions/1`, `refund/2`, `manage_subscriptions/0` and `redeem_offer/1`; introductory offers in the StoreKit configuration (`intro:`).
- `CauldronNative.Device.request_review/0`: the App Store's rating prompt, and Google Play's in-app review.
- `CauldronNative.Weather.Report` holds the cloud cover, whether rain is coming within the hour, and the next 25 hours.
- The Xcode project carries the development team, so it opens in Xcode ready to sign; a device build that fails on an unregistered App Group says how to register it.
- Android apps place a map of their own with `CauldronMapView`.

- `CauldronNative.Weather`: the weather now and today at a place, from WeatherKit on Apple's systems with `weatherkit: true` in the config, and from Open-Meteo on Android and wherever WeatherKit cannot answer, as `CauldronNative.Weather.Report` with the attribution each source requires.
- Links: `url_schemes:` in the config registers the app's URL schemes (URL types on Apple's systems, an intent filter on Android); a link opened arrives as `{:cauldron_native, :url, url}`, also when it starts the app.
- `CauldronNative.Background`: the system wakes the app to refresh its data (`background_refresh: true`): `BGTaskScheduler` on iOS and tvOS, `NSBackgroundActivityScheduler` on macOS, WorkManager on Android, which starts the app's BEAM without its activity. The wake arrives as `{:cauldron_native, :background, :refresh}`.
- `CauldronNative.Widget`: Home Screen widgets from `CauldronNative.UI` controls, named in the config (`widgets:`): a WidgetKit extension built as a second target of the Xcode project, sharing an App Group with the app, and Glance widgets on Android with their receivers and provider resources generated (`CauldronNative.Build.Widgets`, `CauldronNative.Build.AndroidWidgets`). Material icons are drawn into Android's widgets and notification icons.
- `CauldronNative.LiveActivity`: start, update and finish something the user follows outside the app (`live_activities: true`): a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, and on Android a Live Update, an ongoing `ProgressStyle` notification the system promotes to a status bar chip on Android 16 QPR1 and later. Activities are found by their tag after the app restarts.
- `CauldronNative.Purchases`: in-app purchases with StoreKit and Google Play Billing, products named in the config (`purchases:`) as consumables, non-consumables or subscriptions; products with the store's names and prices (`CauldronNative.Purchases.Product`), buying, what the user owns, restoring, and being told when that changes. The Xcode project carries a StoreKit configuration of the products and a scheme that runs the app with it (`CauldronNative.Build.StoreKit`).
- `CauldronNative.UI.dialog/4`: a modal dialog with the app's own controls (a sheet on iOS, tvOS and macOS, Material's `AlertDialog` on Android).
- HTTPS from the device: the system's certificate authorities are loaded at start (`CauldronNative.Certificates`: Android's store, and on iOS and tvOS the `castore` bundle; the Mac's own are read by `:public_key`), and `:public_key` and `:crypto` start with the app.
- iOS and tvOS look up host names with Elixir's resolver against the network's DNS servers, read with `libresolv` and followed as the network changes, as the system forbids the helper program the emulator otherwise starts.
- `CauldronNative.UI.split/3` (a sidebar beside a detail pane: SwiftUI's split view, Material's list and detail panes), `table/3` (SwiftUI's table, collapsing to rows on a phone; rows of columns or list rows on Android) and `theme/2` (light, dark or the system's, an accent colour, and Android's Dynamic colour or a whole Material set of colours derived from the accent).
- Any control takes `on_tap`; buttons take `style: :icon`; labels, buttons, menus, list rows and tabs take `ios:` and `android:` icon names; Material's extended icons are in the Android app.
- An app whose screens read processes of its own starts in its own supervision tree after them: `use CauldronNative.App` gives it a `child_spec/1`, and off the device the runtime returns `:ignore`.
- `CauldronNative.Device.system/0` says which system the app runs on.
- On Android, text before a spacer in a row wraps in the spacer's room, as SwiftUI does, so a trailing button stays in view; forms on the Mac are grouped, as System Settings' are.
- The package is `cauldron_native` (was `cauldron_mobile`), as it builds Mac and Apple TV apps as well as phone ones: modules `CauldronNative.*`, the task `mix cauldron_native.build`, the project config key `:cauldron_native`, events `{:cauldron_native, kind, payload}`, the environment variables `CAULDRON_NATIVE_TEAM`, `CAULDRON_NATIVE_DATA` and `CAULDRON_NATIVE_LOG`, the runtime cache `~/.cache/cauldron_native`, and the guide "Building apps".
- Foldables: `{:cauldron_native, :posture, %{fold, angle, hinge}}` and `CauldronNative.posture/0` report how the iPhone Duo (UIKit's hinge, iOS 27.1) and Android foldables (Jetpack WindowManager and the hinge angle sensor) are folded, with the fold's place in the window on Android.
- `mix cauldron_native.build ios --simulator` and `tvos --simulator` build for Apple's simulators and install and start the app in the one running or the one `--device` names (`CauldronNative.Build.Simulator`); OpenSSL and OTP are cross-compiled for the simulator.
- tvOS apps: `mix cauldron_native.build tvos`, the same SwiftUI controls with tvOS's own where it lacks iOS's (menu pickers for dates, −/+ buttons for steppers, a focusable bar for sliders, an expanding row for disclosures, the top tab bar), Apple TV's layered icon and top shelf images from the app's icon (`CauldronNative.Build.TvIcon`), and `{:error, :unsupported}` for what an Apple TV has no part for (pickers, camera, recording, notifications shown). The emulator is compiled without `fork`, which tvOS forbids.
- macOS apps: `mix cauldron_native.build macos` makes a Mac app of the same SwiftUI screens in an AppKit window with the standard menus, and of a game's screen; files and photos come from the open panel, sharing from the share menu. The Mac app carries the emulator's helper programs, so `System.cmd/3` and the system's name lookup work there, and keeps its files under its own Application Support folder.
- `Build.Ios` is now `CauldronNative.Build.Apple`, building for iOS, tvOS and macOS; each platform's build is kept in its own folder under `_build/<system>_<env>/cauldron_native/`.
- Dependencies' static NIF builds are given `APPLE_SDK` and `APPLE_TARGET` (`CauldronNative.Build.Nifs`); `CAULDRON_TARGET` is the system: `ios`, `tvos`, `macos` or `android`.

- The phone's position (`CauldronNative.Location`): one reading or readings as it moves, from Core Location and Android's location manager, as `CauldronNative.Location.Reading`.
- Notifications (`CauldronNative.Notifications`): the app's own, now or later, answered when tapped even when the tap starts the app; push through Apple's push service and Firebase Cloud Messaging with the app's `:push` config, the token and messages arriving as answers.
- `CauldronNative.UI.chart/2` and `series/3` (Swift Charts; Vico on Android): bars, lines, areas, points, pies and rings; `map/1` and `marker/4` (MapKit; Google Maps with the app's `:maps` key), with markers, the phone's position and the view's moves.
- The stores: the app's `:icon` (an adaptive icon on Android), `:launch` screen (held until the first screen on Android), `:build` number, `:privacy` (iOS's privacy manifest, `CauldronNative.Build.Privacy`), `:encryption` and `:android_signing`; `mix cauldron_native.build --release` makes the `.ipa` for App Store Connect and the App Bundle for Google Play, and `mix cauldron_native.upload_key` the Play upload key. The guide "Publishing" covers the steps around them.
- TalkBack reads a control's `hint` as its action ("double tap to …").
- Android apps follow the phone's DNS servers as the network changes (`CauldronNative.Network`).
- Answers that arrive before the app's process is running wait for it.
- A build removes the same platform's builds from the project's other environments, and the runtime cache keeps only the current OTP version.

- The phone's services for apps (`CauldronNative.Device`): haptics, the photo picker, the system camera for photos and videos, the document picker, recording from the microphone, permission prompts, the share sheet, opening addresses in other apps, and audio that plays on with the screen locked, shown with the lock screen's controls. Answers come to `handle_info/2` as `{:device, tag, answer}`. The app's config names its `:permissions`, each with the reason shown, and `:background_audio`; the build writes them into `Info.plist` and the Android manifest.
- `CauldronNative.UI.video/2` (AVKit on iOS, Media3 on Android) and `camera_preview/1` (AVFoundation on iOS, CameraX on Android).
- Tabs adapt to the screen: a tab bar that opens into a sidebar on an iPad, and a navigation rail in place of the bottom bar on a large Android screen.

- Android apps may use the network (`INTERNET` and `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`); host names resolve through Elixir's own resolver against the network's DNS servers, so a lookup no longer needs a helper program, whose absence stopped the runtime.
- Controls for apps: icons, images, labels, links, menus, chips, pickers, date and time pickers, steppers, progress, many-line text, cards, grids, expandable sections, tabs, toolbars, search, sheets, alerts and action sheets; buttons take an icon, and buttons in lists are the platform's list rows. A picker's and tabs' values are the app's own terms, a date picker's a `Date`, `Time` or `NaiveDateTime` (`CauldronNative.UI.Value`).
- Apps with native controls: `CauldronNative.App` and `CauldronNative.UI` describe screens in Elixir that SwiftUI (iOS) and Jetpack Compose with Material 3 (Android) draw, sending the platform only the controls that changed (`CauldronNative.UI.Tree`) and the app the controls' events. Views of the app's own in Swift and Kotlin under `native/` are compiled in and placed with `CauldronNative.UI.native/2`. Such an app keeps the status bar and follows the device's light or dark setting. The Android app is built with Kotlin, Compose and AndroidX.
- First release: `CauldronNative` (the screen, overlay, touches and lifecycle of an app), and `mix cauldron_native.build` for iOS and Android.
- Game controllers on iOS (GameController) and Android (gamepad key and motion events) arrive as `{:cauldron_native, :pad, event}`; `CauldronNative.quit/0` closes an Android app.
- Nothing is logged on the device: the default log handler is off, output is not forwarded, and crashes write no dump.
- `CauldronNative.gpu_surface/0` and `hide_gpu_surface/0`: a surface for a renderer that draws with the graphics card, a Metal layer on iOS and a Vulkan window on Android. iOS apps need iOS 18 or later.
- `:files` and `:env` in the app's config copy data into the app and set environment variables before the BEAM starts; `--log` keeps Elixir's log on for a development build, and on Android sends the runtime's output to logcat (tag `cauldron`), which `--run` shows.
- On-screen buttons draw their label on two lines, the button's name over its action, fitted inside the circle; Android draws them as iOS does.
